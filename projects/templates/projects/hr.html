Objective

This was a personal project where I set out to design and implement a multi-page HR analytics dashboard in Microsoft Power BI. The objective was to create an end-to-end reporting solution that provided a holistic view of the workforce and could simulate the type of dashboard an HR department might use. The dashboard analyzed multiple dimensions of human resources, including employee attrition, compensation, job levels, education levels, and employee satisfaction. By doing so, it demonstrated how organizations can use data-driven insights to strengthen workforce planning, improve retention strategies, and monitor pay equity across roles and departments.

Dataset

The dataset was structured to represent an organization’s employee records and covered four major categories of information:

  • Employee details such as IDs, job roles, departments, job levels, and education levels.
  • Compensation data including salaries, daily rates, and pay bands.
  • Attrition data that distinguished between active employees and those who had left the organization.
  • Survey-based metrics covering satisfaction and engagement levels across employees.

The raw data required cleaning and structuring before use. It was modeled into fact and dimension tables within Power BI to enable smooth cross-filtering by department, education level, job role, and job band.

Results

The final dashboard consisted of four interactive pages that provided different perspectives of the workforce:

  • HR Overview: presented headcount, attrition rate, average daily rate, salary benchmarks, and workforce distribution by gender, department, and education level.
  • Compensation Breakdown: visualized salary ranges, pay band differences, and equity comparisons across job roles, job levels, and education groups.
  • Attrition Insights: highlighted departments and job levels with the highest turnover, revealing that attrition was concentrated among early-career employees with lower education levels.
  • Employee Insights Overview: allowed drilldowns into demographics, job roles, and satisfaction metrics to uncover patterns in engagement and retention risks.

These results simulated how HR professionals could use such a dashboard to identify retention risks, monitor pay equity, and develop workforce strategies based on data rather than assumptions.

Key Learnings

  • Enhanced technical skills in DAX by building measures for attrition, average tenure, pay equity, and satisfaction index.
  • Learned how to design multi-page dashboards that balance high-level executive KPIs with detailed drilldowns for HR managers.
  • Understood how job roles, education, and job levels influence both attrition and compensation outcomes.
  • Practiced dashboard storytelling, structuring insights to guide decision-makers from a high-level overview down to specific workforce patterns.
